Game Mode

The Bottom Dock

Game Mode's new main menu. Live cards rise above each item: continue playing, friends online, downloads with progress, nearby devices. Over a game it becomes the game's dock, and its search finds settings too.

In game

A top-dock overlay

Now playing, Performance (temperatures, power draw, per-game saving), Controller layout, Friends with quick replies, Capture and Quick settings. A short Guide tap shows a quick-glance bar without pausing.

Settings

Settings, redesigned

Six groups with live status cards and pages as tabs you flip with LB RB. Every earlier setting is still there.

Experimental

Qt renderer

Settings › Display › Renderer › Qt draws Home, the Library, game pages, Downloads, Quick Access, Power and Settings natively with Qt Quick. If it fails, Hearth falls back to Automatic.

Hearth Disks

A USB maker

Under the hood

An update can't break your system

Hearth OS is image-based: the whole system is one tested image that updates in one piece. It can't half-install, and the version before stays on the drive.

  1. It downloads in the backgroundThe new image comes down while you play, as one piece.
  2. You restart into itWhenever suits you. Your games, saves and files stay as they are.
  3. The last version staysIt waits in the boot menu, and in Settings › System › Updates.
  4. Something wrong? Go backPick it at start-up, or from the Recovery USB when nothing starts.

Editions

Two editions, the same Hearth

Hearth looks and works the same on both. What changes is the Linux underneath. You can switch later without a USB stick.

Main edition

Hearth OS on Anatase

Lean and image-based, from people who worked on Bazzite: Fedora underneath. One ISO for AMD, Intel and Nvidia, with Handheld Daemon built in. Every change is tested here first.

Beta Experimental

Hearth OS on Peppermint

Peppermint OS on Debian 13, made into an image, for people who like Debian's steady pace.

The Ubuntu and CachyOS editions are archived for now while Anatase and Peppermint get polished. They'll be back. Systems on them can switch from Settings › Updates.

Why Hearth left Bazzite, and moving from Hearth OS on Bazzite
  • Too much it doesn't use: Bazzite's Game Mode setup, KDE, tools and services cost space, memory and start-up time.
  • Heavier than it should be: 8 to 10 GB installs, bigger updates, sluggish on Nvidia.
  • Two of everything: a separate Nvidia image and ISO; Anatase is one for every PC.
  • Better handhelds: Handheld Daemon, shown in Hearth's own screens.
  • Hearth OS on Bazzite is archived (0.1.3 was its last update). Move from Settings › Updates › Move to a new Hearth OS; games and saves stay. All editions install from the small installer, Anatase also from the offline USB.

Installing from the ISO

  1. Write it to a USB stick16 GB or more, with Fedora Media Writer, balenaEtcher or Hearth Disks.
  2. Boot from the USBBoot menu: often F12, F11 or F8. ROG Ally: hold Volume Down at power-on.
  3. Try it outThe live system starts in Hearth with a welcome card. Nothing changes yet.
  4. Choose Install HearthPick a drive and restart when it's done.

Hearth Recovery USB

Fix a Hearth device that won't start: works with only the controller or the touchscreen. On handhelds, boot it with the firmware boot menu (ROG Ally: hold Volume Down + Power), then pick Start the previous version. Write it with Hearth Disks, Rufus in DD mode or Ventoy; step by step.

SHA-256: hearth-recovery.iso.sha256

Recovery USB about 5 GB

Switch from Bazzite, Fedora Atomic or another image-based system

Bazzite, Bluefin, Aurora, Silverblue or Kinoite switch with one command, no USB stick. Your account and files stay, and the old system stays in the boot menu. Beta Experimental: back up first. Peppermint can't be switched to this way; use the small installer.

From Bazzite, Bluefin, Aurora or Fedora Atomic

sudo bootc switch ghcr.io/dragongamer9/hearth-os:latest
Older system without bootc, or going back 
sudo rpm-ostree rebase ostree-unverified-registry:ghcr.io/dragongamer9/hearth-os:latest

To go back, pick the previous boot entry or run sudo bootc rollback. Hearth OS updates itself from Settings › Updates.

From Hearth OS on Bazzite

Settings › Updates › Move to a new Hearth OS keeps your games, saves and store libraries (the rest of home is set aside for 30 days). Or in Desktop Mode:

pkexec /usr/libexec/nimbus/hearth-admin move-to-new-os

This is a beta. Hearth OS and Ember aren't ready to be your everyday system yet, and things will break now and then. Updates come out almost daily and install themselves. Keep a backup, and tell us what breaks.

Questions

What hardware does it need?

Steam Deck, ROG Ally, Legion Go and most x86-64 desktops and laptops with AMD, Intel or Nvidia (GTX 16 / RTX 20 and newer) graphics. For installing, a USB stick of 16 GB or more and a drive of 64 GB or more.

Can I go back?

Yes. Updates and switches keep the previous system in the boot menu; pick it, use Settings › System › Updates, or run sudo bootc rollback. If nothing starts, the Hearth Recovery USB can start the previous version.

Does it work with anti-cheat games?

Nothing is injected into games; the overlay is an ordinary window. Couch Co-op doesn't support anti-cheat games.

Do I need two Hearth devices for Hearth Link?

Hearth Link is for copying games and keeping Hearth in sync between your own Hearth devices, so yes. Friends, chat and Play together work with one.

Is it really free?

Yes. Hearth OS is free and open source under the GPL-3.0. Games you buy come from the stores as usual.

Something broke. How do I report it?

Send logs from Settings › System › Help & logs or with hearth-send-logs, and tell us what happened.
