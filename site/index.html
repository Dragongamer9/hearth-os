Hearth Input

Play games that have no controller support: a game's controller layout turns the controller into a keyboard and mouse while you play. Ready-made layouts for shooters, strategy and retro games, or make your own with tap and hold, a second set of buttons, gyro aiming and the back paddles.

Quick Resume that shows the right game

Every game now gets its own number, the way SteamOS does it, so switching between games put aside always brings back the one you picked.

Every Handheld Daemon setting

Controller mode, gyro, back buttons, power, fan and lights, all inside Hearth in its own look. Quick Access over games is drawn through Handheld Daemon now, and it's much smoother.

Mantel, a full desktop

Pin apps to the taskbar, snap windows to halves and corners, Alt+Tab, four desktops, icons on the desktop, a calendar and notification history, and new Settings for date and time, keyboard layouts, default apps, users and printers. Plus an Apps store for Flathub and clipboard history on Super+V.

Sign in on the desktop

Steam stays out of sight

Two new ways to play together, both Beta Experimental: they're new and haven't been tested on real devices yet, so expect rough edges and send logs from Settings › System when something goes wrong.

Couch Co-op Beta Experimental

Split-screen for 2 to 4 players, even in games that don't have it: every player gets their own part of the screen, their own controller and their own copy of the game, which find each other like on a local network. Each player just presses A to join. Games with anti-cheat aren't supported.

Multi-seat Beta Experimental

One PC, several consoles: each screen or TV gets its own Game Mode with its own controllers, Steam account, games and saves. Works with a graphics card per player, or one card driving several screens. One button puts everything back.

Fixes

Game Mode

Made for a controller from the first screen

Game Mode runs fullscreen in gamescope, the same compositor SteamOS uses. Everything works with the D-pad, sticks and face buttons, with an on-screen keyboard whenever you need to type.

  • One library for every store. Install, play and uninstall Epic and GOG games without their launchers.
  • Quick Access over any game. Press the Xbox button for volume, brightness, power limit, frame-rate limit, screenshots and open apps. The game keeps running underneath and doesn't see your button presses.
  • Quick Resume, like an Xbox. Put a game aside and jump into another; up to five stay paused in memory and carry on right where you left them.
  • Cloud saves for Epic and GOG, plus your Steam friends list: who's online and what they're playing.
  • Profiles for the whole household. Everyone gets their own Steam account, sign-ins and play time, with an optional PIN and parental controls, while sharing the installed games.
  • Works offline. Game art is saved on the device, so your library looks the same on a plane.

Mantel

A real desktop, still in reach of your thumbs

Mantel is Hearth's desktop. Switch to it from Game Mode for browsing, mods, files and anything else, then hop back with one button. No logging out.

  • Its own settings app for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, sound, display, power, controller and updates.
  • The controller works as a mouse and keyboard everywhere on the desktop.
  • Power limit and power modes you can set yourself, plus volume up to 150% when you turn it on.
  • Wallpapers made for Hearth, including moving ones.
  • Hearth Browser, Hearth's own web browser on Chromium, with tabs, bookmarks, history and downloads.
  • Task Manager and Files, made for a controller: end stuck apps, watch performance, and move your files, games and SD cards around.
  • A lock screen and login screen you can use with a controller, or skip altogether: you choose whether it asks for a password.
  • On Windows too: Ember's Mantel is the same desktop, with the same taskbar, launcher, Quick Access and Hearth menu.

Under the hood

A system that looks after itself

Hearth OS is an image-based system: the whole OS updates as one piece, and if something goes wrong you boot the previous version.

Updates that roll back

System updates land in the background and take effect on the next boot. A bad one is undone by picking the previous entry.

Handhelds and desktops

Built on Anatase, with Handheld Daemon for the ROG Ally, Legion Go and Steam Deck, and one image for desktop PCs with AMD, Intel or Nvidia graphics.

Your power, your call

Pick a power mode, set the power limit in watts and cap the frame rate, for the whole system or per game. Hearth remembers it across restarts.

Remap your controller

Change what each button does and save layouts, right from Settings.

Sign in your way

Each store's own sign-in page, with the on-screen keyboard and controller mouse built in.

Nothing injected

The overlay is an ordinary window on top, so anti-cheat has nothing to object to.

Its own installer

Install next to Windows or on a whole drive, with a controller or a mouse. Then Hearth offers to bring your Steam, Epic, GOG and Lutris games over.

Password or not

Sign in automatically like a console, or ask for a password at start and after sleep. Your call, and you can change it any time.

Hearth Browser

A web browser in Hearth's look, built on Chromium, that blocks ads and trackers. Your bookmarks, history and tabs stay on your device.

Coming next

A new foundation: moving from Bazzite to Anatase

Hearth OS has been built on Bazzite, and Bazzite got it a long way. But Hearth brings its own Game Mode, desktop and settings, and underneath them Bazzite carries a lot Hearth doesn't use. So Hearth OS is moving to Anatase, a lean, image-based system from people who worked on Bazzite.

Why we're leaving Bazzite

  • Too much we don't use. Bazzite ships its own Steam Game Mode setup, KDE desktop, tools and services. Hearth replaces most of them, but they still take space, memory and start-up time on every device.
  • Heavier and slower than it should be. The system image is big (an install downloads 8 to 10 GB), updates are larger, and Hearth felt sluggish on top of it, especially on Nvidia.
  • Two versions of everything. Bazzite needs a separate image and ISO for Nvidia. Anatase is one image for AMD, Intel and Nvidia, so there's one download for every PC.
  • Better handheld support. Anatase uses Handheld Daemon for controllers, gyro, back buttons, fans and power limits, which Hearth can show in its own screens.

Installing from the ISO

  1. Write the ISO to a USB stick (16 GB or more) with Fedora Media Writer or balenaEtcher.
  2. Boot from the USB. On a desktop, open the boot menu (often F12, F11 or F8 at power-on). On a ROG Ally, hold Volume Down while powering on.
  3. Try it out. The live system starts in Hearth with a welcome card. Nothing is changed yet.
  4. Choose Install Hearth, pick a drive and restart when it's done.

Switching from Bazzite, Fedora Atomic or another image-based system

Already on an image-based Fedora system (Bazzite, Bluefin, Aurora, Silverblue, Kinoite)? You can switch it to Hearth OS on Anatase with one command, no USB stick. Your account and your files stay, and the system you had stays in the boot menu, so you can go back. Beta Experimental: back up anything important first.

From Hearth OS on Bazzite

Open Settings › Updates › Move to a new Hearth OS and pick Hearth OS on Anatase. It's a clean move that keeps your games, their saves and your Steam, Epic and GOG libraries, and puts the rest of your home folder aside for 30 days. Or, in a terminal in Desktop Mode:

pkexec /usr/libexec/nimbus/hearth-admin move-to-new-os

From Bazzite, Bluefin, Aurora or Fedora Atomic

In a terminal, then restart:

sudo bootc switch ghcr.io/dragongamer9/hearth-os:latest

On an older system without bootc:

sudo rpm-ostree rebase ostree-unverified-registry:ghcr.io/dragongamer9/hearth-os:latest

Changed your mind? Pick the previous entry in the boot menu, or run sudo bootc rollback and restart.

Hearth OS updates itself from then on (Settings › Updates). The Ubuntu, CachyOS and Peppermint editions are a different base, so they can't be switched to from Fedora; they install from the small installer once it's available.

This is a beta. Hearth OS and Ember are still being built and aren't ready for everyday use yet. Things will break now and then. If you use it as your main system anyway, thank you, and please be patient: I try to put out updates every day, and they install by themselves from Settings › Updates. Keep a backup of anything important before installing, and tell us what breaks.
